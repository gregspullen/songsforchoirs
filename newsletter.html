Hello Choristers,

Trick or treat?

We have a treat for you this month, but more of that shortly.

First, some welcome news from the studio. Everything is now up and running, and we're once again able to produce our demonstration recordings, provided the usual studio gremlins remain reasonably cooperative.

You may have wondered why there hasn't been quite as much new music appearing on our metaphorical shelves lately. The answer is that we've been rather busy behind the scenes. Our sacred music catalogue now has a complete set of new covers, and there's been a good deal of technical work on the website. With luck, you won't have noticed the latter at all, which is rather the point.

Meanwhile, Lyra Harker's composing career continues its quiet progress. Bathed in Starlight has been attracting a pleasing amount of attention on YouTube, despite our almost complete absence of promotional activity. Perhaps one day we'll discover whether we're any good at marketing. For the moment, we're simply delighted that people are finding the music.

Three new additions to the catalogue

Popular SATB Works, Volume One

Our first collection brings together six SATB works in a single volume, offering choirs an economical way to explore a selection of Lyra's music.

We haven't yet decided whether this will become a permanent fixture in the catalogue, but while it's available, there's an especially attractive option for Patrons.

An annual Patron membership costs £12 and entitles you to half-price music purchased directly from Songs for Choirs. With membership, ten copies of the collection cost just £20, bringing the total to £32 — a saving of £8 on the regular collection price. And, of course, your Patron discount continues for the remainder of your twelve-month membership.

Hallelujah!

Handel's magnificent Hallelujah Chorus, reimagined in a shorter, more accessible arrangement.

We've managed to fit the whole thing into less than two minutes without losing its essential character or exuberance. An opportunity to enjoy one of the most celebrated choruses in the repertoire without quite so many pages to turn.

Thine Own Son

This is our latest original work, and one of which I'm particularly proud.

It takes its inspiration from the Nunc Dimittis, the Song of Simeon, but approaches the familiar words from a rather different perspective.

I imagined Simeon holding the infant Jesus and singing his prayer as a lullaby: a moment of profound peace and fulfilment, shadowed by his warning to Mary that a sword shall pierce her own soul.

Years later, at the foot of the cross, Mary remembers those words. What was once a song of comfort becomes a mother's prayer as she watches her son suffer and die.

The music follows that journey from tenderness to grief, allowing the same words to acquire an entirely different meaning. Although it draws on the Nunc Dimittis, it is not intended as a conventional liturgical setting, and the Gloria Patri is omitted.

It is a work for Candlemas, Holy Week, or any occasion where its themes of love, loss and acceptance may resonate.

What comes next?

I wish I could tell you. We don't tend to plan our compositions very far in advance. Usually, an idea presents itself, refuses to go away, and eventually becomes a piece of music. We'll see what arrives next.

In the meantime, thank you, as always, for visiting the website, listening to the recordings and, most importantly, singing the music.

There is something enormously rewarding about seeing these pieces finding their way into the world and being appreciated on their own merits. We're grateful to everyone who helps make that possible.

Until next time,

Gregory
