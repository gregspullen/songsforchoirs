A Note from the Pond – October 2026

Hello Choristers,

Welcome to October!

I must begin by apologising for writing nothing last month. The house move completely dominated the intervening weeks, but we are now established in our new home, along with what appears to be a lifetime supply of cardboard boxes.

The studio has been reassembled and is mostly functional, although we have yet to put it to any real work.

I am delighted to see that Lyra is beginning to find an audience in her own right. Her works are starting to appear in increasingly far-flung places, not least on YouTube, where Bathed in Starlight has proved particularly popular despite our policy of concentrating on writing music rather than promoting it. Which brings me rather neatly to the subject of this newsletter.

We have been considering ways to promote the catalogue. Considering things, of course, achieves remarkably little by itself, but we have at least been busy tidying up the website and making some fairly substantial changes behind the scenes. Most recently we have changed our internet and email providers, which involved a surprising amount of coding rather than the occasional phone call and email I had rather naively anticipated. These changes should make things more efficient and, in time, allow us to expand a little more easily.

As I am firstly a musician, I had no idea just how much work could be involved in an apparently simple job such as adding a copyright phrase to the bottom of every preview. The job got done. Nobody really benefits from it directly, but details like that help us to maintain and improve our professional image in a highly competitive market, and demonstrate that we are trying to create the highest-quality product we can.

At His Name

The new addition to the catalogue this month is At His Name. This was originally composed around twenty years ago as part of an oratorio, The Painter and the Patriarch, inspired by the Zurbarán paintings displayed at Auckland Castle in Bishop Auckland, County Durham. The words are by David Napthine and could perhaps best be described as an apocryphal psalm.

Although At His Name was never intended for liturgical use, I think it can nevertheless work as a powerful and contemplative concert piece. The text becomes increasingly uncomfortable as it progresses, drawing on some of the more troubling imagery associated with sacrifice, conflict and divine power. Revisiting it after twenty years, I considered whether some of that should be softened. In the end I decided that its discomfort was part of the point, and something to preserve rather than remove.

Revisiting At His Name has also encouraged me to look again at some of the other music which has been sitting quietly on the shelf for rather too long. There are several pieces which may yet find their way into the Songs for Choirs catalogue, alongside the new works which Lyra continues to produce.

With the move finally behind us, the studio back together and most of the cardboard boxes gradually disappearing, I am hoping that October might involve rather more music and rather less plumbing, coding and administration.

In the meantime, thank you for continuing to visit the site, listen to the music and, most importantly, sing it. It is enormously encouraging to see pieces beginning to find their way into the world without too much shouting from us.

Until next time,

Gregory
